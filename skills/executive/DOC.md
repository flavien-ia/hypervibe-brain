# /brain:executive

Keeps in your brain **what your AI knows how to do**: your own skills, and the guards that frame them. It is the executive floor of the brain, beside the floor of knowledge (your notes). Claude Code finds them where it always looks for them, and another AI plugged into your brain finds them too.

## When to use it

- You wrote skills of your own and want them to live in your brain, saved and synchronised with it
- You have guards (hooks that ask or refuse before a risky step) and want the AI never to be able to change them
- You set your brain up on a new computer and want your skills and guards there
- You want to know where the executive floor stands: what is in, what is left in Claude Code's folders, what is no longer in place
- You want to switch off a skill of yours, or a whole plugin, without losing it: "switch off the skill X", "switch the harness Y off", and later "switch X on again"

## How it goes

1. **Where it stands**: Claude tells you how many skills and guards already live in your brain, what is left in Claude Code's folders, and what is not in place.
2. **The choice**: Claude offers to bring in what is left, and you choose what (all, some, none).
3. **Bringing in**: each skill chosen moves into your brain, a copy is kept first, and a link stays in its place: you call it by its /command as before.
4. **The guards**: Claude asks them a few harmless cases before and after the move, to check they answer the same. A guard that calls a file beside it (a confirmation window) moves with that file.
5. **On a new computer**: Claude puts back the links of your skills and wires your guards again. Your skills are there at the next session. What you switched off stays off.
6. **Switching off and on**: you say what to switch off, Claude does it and tells you when it takes effect. A skill of yours leaves Claude Code at once and stays in your brain; a plugin goes off at your next session. If the plugin checks some actions before they happen, Claude asks you first, since that protection goes with it.

## What it creates for you

- An `executive/` folder in your brain: your skills, your guards, and how each is wired
- Your skills, called as before, but each improvement of them is an improvement of your brain
- Guards the AI can neither change nor move: only you change them
- In the live view, the **Harnesses** layer: your skills and your plugins as a crown around the tools, a skill lighting up when the AI uses it
- A copy of everything moved, in the backups of your computer
- The list of what you switched off, kept in your brain: it follows you to another computer, and another AI plugged into your brain leaves those skills out too
- A warning when one of your skills seems to hold a key or a password (where it is, never its value), so that you can move it to a safer place

## Prerequisites

- A brain already created (`/brain:create`)
- Claude Code, with its skills and hooks in its usual folders

## Tips

- The plugins you install are not moved: each is a product, with its own repository. The view shows them beside your skills.
- To change a guard, open its file yourself: the AI has no access to it, that is the point.
- `/brain:status` tells you when a skill or a guard is no longer in place.
- Three words: a **skill** is know-how written down, which your AI follows when you call it; a **harness** is a plugin you installed, a set of skills with sometimes its own checks; a **guard** is a check of your own that frames your AI.
- In the live view, the switch is in the card of each of your skills and of each harness (click the name of its arc). What is off stays drawn, faint.
- One skill of a plugin cannot be switched off alone: Claude Code has no setting for it. The whole plugin can.
- A guard has no switch, for your AI or in the live view: to pause one, you do it by hand. Your brain's own plugin cannot be switched off from the brain either, it carries the locks.
- Another AI plugged into your brain (Codex, Cursor, Claude Desktop...) gets your skills from the brain itself, translated for it. Your guards cannot stop its steps: it receives them as rules, and asks you first.
